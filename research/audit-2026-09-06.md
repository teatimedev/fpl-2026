# FPL project audit — 6 September 2026

This project has a useful statistical and optimisation foundation, but has not yet demonstrated an elite FPL decision-making edge. Its biggest opportunity is to improve the reliability of its information and prove the value of its complete strategy. More AI, datasets and simulations should support that objective; their quantity is not a measure of progress.

This was an investigation, not an implementation. Production code, parameters, deployments and the FPL account were left unchanged. The accompanying [evidence file](/Users/jordanrippon/projects/fpl-2026/research/audit-2026-09-06/evidence.json) contains the official score reconciliation and production snapshot; [rendered dashboard evidence](/Users/jordanrippon/projects/fpl-2026/research/audit-2026-09-06/deployed-dashboard.txt) records the observed gameweek mismatch.

The audit covered the v1/v2 documentation, production player/team models, historical validation, minutes and availability, news/market ingestion, optimisation, transfers, chips, simulation, scorecard, relevant research modules, frontend decision logic and current GitHub/deployment state. Independent reviewers examined the modelling, decisions and pipeline. All 224 existing Python tests passed; the app build and lint passed. **What the goal should mean**

An unbeatable team is not achievable in a stochastic game. A useful ambitious goal is a system that repeatedly makes better decisions with the information available at each deadline, maximises season-long expected points and measures whether it actually improves against credible alternatives.

Three objectives need separate treatment: maximising expected season points, maximising the probability of winning a particular mini-league, and maximising the very small probability of finishing first overall. They can prefer different risks. A mini-league strategy needs rival squads, captaincy, chip inventory, points gaps and remaining weeks. Finishing first overall is not achieved simply by buying low-owned players. At this early stage, expected points and flexibility are sensible defaults; later, target-specific simulations can quantify when accepting lower expected points buys meaningful win probability.

The current project optimises expected points over a rolling window, with heuristic values for transfers and chips. It does not yet represent the field or optimise any of those competition probabilities.

**What deserves keeping**

- Team attack/defence ratings and bookmaker match odds are a better foundation than relying on FPL's coarse fixture-difficulty labels.
- Shrinking small samples and using xG/xA are sensible principles. Avoiding a knee-jerk reaction to one blank or a defender's unlikely goal is valuable.
- The minutes model distinguishes starts and cameos and reacts to recent selection. The direction of its improvement survives the data-quality sensitivity check below.
- The optimiser respects squad composition, club limits and legal formations. Transfer advice measures effects on the scoring XI, captaincy and bench coverage, instead of simply comparing two player projections.
- Comparing a transfer now with a path that waits is a useful way to value flexibility.
- Shared simulated club outcomes, formation-aware autosubs, source-linked availability overrides and archived predictions provide useful components for a stronger system.

These should be improved and unified, rather than discarded in a wholesale rewrite.

The more relevant totals harness is useful, but still assigns historical players to their **current** clubs and retains older cameo/aging calculations that differ from production. Current forward evidence is only two finished GWs. The model's starter rank correlation averages 0.341 and MAE 2.48. Better ranking and better absolute accuracy are different properties. The two-GW model mean must not be compared with the one-GW external mean as though they cover the same observations.

Build a deadline replay harness that calls the actual production functions, using historically correct players, clubs, prices, rules, injuries and only information available before each deadline. Tune on earlier periods and keep later periods untouched. Compare full season policies from identical starting states, including captaincy, prices, transfers and chips. Archive immutable inputs and outputs with model versions. Closing odds after an FPL deadline may be useful benchmarks, but cannot be retrospective inputs available to the manager.

**5. Historical data contains false zeros and misleading stability estimates**

In 2022/23 GW1–15, the imported dataset records **zero starts for every player**, despite 2,818 rows with at least 60 minutes. The metric begins being recorded at GW16. [import_gw_history.py:67](/Users/jordanrippon/projects/fpl-2026/v2/import_gw_history.py:67) recognises a missing column but not this period of unmeasured zeros.

The published minutes benchmark reproduces exactly on 107,801 predictions: aggregate Brier 0.118075 versus recency 0.095199. An **in-memory sensitivity**, using minutes ≥60 as an imperfect proxy for the missing labels, changes that to 0.116919 versus 0.098645. Recency still wins in each later season independently. Preserve the approach, repair the evidence, and report the corrected result; this proxy is not a complete historical-label repair.

Shrinkage has a separate problem. [player_model.py:144](/Users/jordanrippon/projects/fpl-2026/v2/player_model.py:144) uses xG/90 reliability of .90 across positions, while shrinking towards position-specific priors. The pooled correlation includes the predictable difference between defenders and forwards. Fresh correlations from the same data are .897 pooled, **.288 for defenders**, .775 for midfielders and .645 for forwards.

For a single 2,200-minute defender season, the current formula assigns about **87%** weight to his own xG rate. Substituting the within-defender correlation would give about **29%**. This is a sensitivity demonstration, not a validated recommendation to use .288 directly. Fit a hierarchical variance model and validate it.

Conversely, DefCon's hardcoded .56 reliability was measured across earlier unrecorded-zero seasons. Restricting to the valid 2024/25→2025/26 pair produces .929 pooled, .774 for defenders and .851 for midfielders, with limited samples. The model risks over-trusting defender attacking history while over-shrinking defensive contribution skill. Measurement availability must be encoded per statistic, source, season and date.

**6. Minutes and point scoring are not yet coherent**

Every start earns two appearance points and the full clean-sheet probability in [player_model.py:785](/Users/jordanrippon/projects/fpl-2026/v2/player_model.py:785). It does not model the chance of reaching 60 minutes. An isolated production-function case with a guaranteed 45-minute starting defender and a guaranteed clean sheet returned **6 points**; correct appearance-plus-clean-sheet scoring is **1**.

This matters even when expected minutes conditional on starting exceed 60: a mean of 75 can include early substitutions. Calafiori had three starts below 60 among 22 starts in the historical 2025/26 sample, while Milenković had none among 37. Expected concession deductions also need the player's on-pitch exposure.

Independent start probabilities fail club constraints. The current local projection file implies Hull 12.80 expected starters, Tottenham 12.45, Coventry 12.14, and Bournemouth 1.40 starting goalkeepers. Marginal probabilities for a full registered squad should sum to 11 and one goalkeeper. Independent estimates can overrate several mutually competing players simultaneously.

Build a joint availability/lineup model plus distributions for starter exits and substitute entry times. Include role, teammate competition, injuries, manager, rest days, international duty and all competitions. Derive appearance, P60, clean sheet and attacking exposure from those scenarios. A regularised statistical model and gradient-boosted challenger are reasonable candidates; use chronological validation to choose them.

**7. Attacking projections can count club strength twice**

[player_model.py:781](/Users/jordanrippon/projects/fpl-2026/v2/player_model.py:781) multiplies a player's historical absolute xG/xA rate by fixture team xG divided by 1.45. A player's historical rate already reflects the quality of the club where it was earned. Applying the entire new club-strength factor again to an established stayer can inflate strong-club players and depress weak-club ones.

The project already tried blunt attenuation and found mixed results. That is not grounds to arbitrarily halve the factor. Recover historical player-club-role stints and compare: opponent adjustments relative to the historical environment; a team-volume model allocating a player's share of opportunities; and a partially pooled hybrid.

Explicitly separate non-penalty xG, penalty opportunities and taker probability. Set-piece orders currently appear in output without a complete separate scoring model. Map xA to FPL-specific assists; model bonus jointly within a match rather than a historical bonus-rate multiplier. Separate uncertainty about ability from normal match variance. Static preseason role multipliers need evidence dates, review triggers and expiry rules.

**9. The simulator is not independent proof of an edge**

[decision_sim.py:365](/Users/jordanrippon/projects/fpl-2026/v2/decision_sim.py:365) calibrates its simulated player means back to our own projections. Therefore “wins in 61% of simulations” means **under this model's assumptions**. It does not show that the model is right or that the transfer has a 61% chance of having superior true expected value. More draws reduce sampling error, not uncertainty about model parameters or omitted information.

Confirmed shortcomings:

- Transfer hit costs are not passed to `compare()`; its delta is raw squad scoring. A +3 raw advantage with a −4 hit is a −1 decision.
- Players draw goals and assists independently conditional on team outcomes, so player events can exceed the team's goals. Bonus is not allocated through a shared match ranking.
- Multiple fixtures are aggregated into one gameweek before player scoring. A keeper playing two clean sheets in an isolated simulation receives **6 instead of 12 points**.
- Whole-window calibration can hide wrong weekly means: a synthetic player's forecast `[3,9]` simulated approximately `[6.05,6.04]` over 200,000 draws. This across-week defect does not apply in the same way to the dashboard's single-GW simulation.
- Captain and vice are chosen by individual mean before fallback is evaluated. For a guaranteed-playing player with 6 expected points and a 50%-appearance player with 5, the latter captained with the former vice has an expected armband contribution of 8; choosing the highest unconditional mean as captain gives 6.

Simulate fixture-level events and complete actions, including hits, chips, future transfers and price changes. Keep each match separate, allocate consistent team/player events and expose model-parameter uncertainty. Check simulated means against each player-fixture forecast, not only the overall horizon. Jointly evaluate legal XI, captain/vice pairs and bench permutations.

**10. Transfer and chip optimisation needs exact state and better future planning**

Owned players are budgeted at current buying prices in [planner.py:104](/Users/jordanrippon/projects/fpl-2026/v2/planner.py:104) and [weekly.py:311](/Users/jordanrippon/projects/fpl-2026/v2/weekly.py:311). Calafiori bought at £5.5m and now priced £5.7m sells for **£5.6m**. With no bank, treating the sale as £5.7m can produce an unaffordable recommendation. Track purchase, sale and current prices per ownership stint.

The wildcard planner carries free transfers forward with **+1** at [planner.py:177](/Users/jordanrippon/projects/fpl-2026/v2/planner.py:177); official rules preserve the previous balance. Its test currently asserts the wrong behaviour. Chips are considered independently, so multiple remaining chips can all be recommended in the last eligible GW, despite one chip per week. Bench Boost value uses gross bench points rather than subtracting the autosub contribution already expected without the chip. [Official rules](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4661029).

There is also historical overconfident wording: on September 2, Triple Captain GW3 was valued at 8.2 versus GW7 at 8.7, yet the recommendation said no better week remained. The condition actually accepts within 10% of the best. The later September 4 forecast values GW3 at 9.3, genuinely the best in that output. This is an earlier rationale defect, not a claim that the latest forecast contradicted itself or that using Haaland TC was demonstrably wrong.

Candidate pruning can remove cheap players needed to fund upgrades: the planner keeps 120 top-total players plus owned players, and pair search keeps 25 per position. Preserve cheapest enablers, price/points Pareto frontiers and fixture-specific leaders; test first-action sensitivity to pool expansion.

After correcting these issues, use a rolling scenario planner with future decisions contingent on information that becomes available. Enforce the same first action across scenarios before that information arrives. Jointly schedule chips on distinct eligible weeks, value wildcard–Bench Boost preparation, and include the residual value of squad structure, bank and free transfers after the chosen horizon. Compare horizons and transfer penalties empirically rather than assuming six weeks and two points per free transfer are universally right. The open [Solio FPL solver](https://github.com/solioanalytics/open-fpl-solver) is a useful deterministic reference implementation to compare against, not automatic proof of stochastic optimality.

**A practical delivery sequence**

| Stage | Deliverable | Acceptance evidence |
|---|---|---|
| First: trusted decisions | Fix GW/version mixing, actual-picks reconciliation, captain prose, sale ledger, wildcard FT rule and chip conflicts | Reproduce each failure; correct official scores and legal affordable actions; rendered GW rollover check |

| Third: information advantage | Working article discovery, source-backed AI claims, validated markets, all-competition minutes, deadline execution | Relevant dated coverage; graded extraction/lineup probabilities; last observation timestamps; no silent zero feeds |
| Fourth: better forecasts | Joint minutes/P60 model, role/stint attack, penalties, calibrated component challengers and ensemble | Out-of-time component and squad decision lift, robust across seasons and positions |
| Fifth: better strategy | Coherent fixture simulations; action-level costs; adaptive transfers and joint chip schedule; optional rival objective | Realised policy comparisons, calibrated uncertainty, affordable plans, first-action stability and regret |

Every candidate change should answer: what information does it add, which decision changes, how much does that decision improve out of sample, and what failure could erase that improvement? AI can also automate experiment generation, error analysis and documentation, but the promotion criterion must be independent results.

# Adding the eye test to the FPL model

Neither Thiago nor Kluivert should be protected by historical projections. The current system lacks a proper channel for observations about movement, technique, role, service and managerial trust. Good xG is evidence of recorded chances; it is not proof that a player is playing well or that the same chances will continue.

This review uses match reports and supporter commentary. I have not watched the full matches. The observations below are attributed to their authors, not presented as my own video scouting. No qualitative downgrade has been applied to production.

## What I found now

**Thiago, Sunderland on 5 September:** Rian Mullaley's VAVEL report gives him 6/10, describes a hard physical battle and seven aerial duels won from fourteen, but says he struggled to find scoring chances. This supports a concern about his threat in this match, alongside positive evidence about effort. It does not establish a persistent loss of ability or a change in selection prospects. [Report](https://www.vavel.com/en/football/2026/09/05/premier-league/1270303-brentford-1-1-sunderland-post-match-brentford-player-ratings.html)

The Brentford supporter thread contains dissatisfaction with the team's first half, a comment praising Thiago's effort while questioning the missing goals, and another arguing last season's returns were inflated by penalties and fortunate goals. Those are hypotheses from a small, self-selected sample, not verified consensus. [Supporter discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/Brentford/comments/1w84qs4/postmatch_thread_brentford_11_sunderland_premier/)

Brentford's own report provides counterevidence to a claim that he offered no threat: a saved header, the cross preceding a disallowed goal, and defenders cutting out late service before it reached him. These incidents warrant distinguishing poor finishing, poor service and good defending. A club report also has its own editorial incentives. [Club report](https://www.brentfordfc.com/en/news/article/match-reports-brentford-1-sunderland-1-premier-league-vitaly-janelt-05-09-2026)

**Kluivert, Newcastle on 5 September:** Opta's report describes his long-range attempt striking the post before a later cross produced Bournemouth's second goal. His low FPL score therefore does not mean he contributed nothing. Equally, an exciting long-range attempt is not automatically evidence of repeatable high-quality opportunities. [Opta report](https://theanalyst.com/articles/newcastle-vs-bournemouth-stats-premier-league-09-2026)

The conclusion from this limited review is concern worth investigating, especially for Thiago's supply and threat against Sunderland. It is not yet a substantiated multi-match diagnosis that he has become a poor player. Player quality, current performance and FPL value at the available price must remain distinct assessments.

## The actual project gap

`v2/news_extract.py` recognises explicit absences, fitness tests, return dates, availability and predicted starts/benches through regular expressions. It does not extract performance or tactical observations. Even predicted-lineup adjustments are currently review-only in `v2/news_pipeline.py`. Adding another injury feed will not solve this gap.

## Proposed scouting process

1. **Collect observations for the squad and replacement candidates.** Use club beat reporters, tactical analysts, thoughtful club supporters, opposition observers and accessible match footage. For podcasts/videos, distinguish what is explicitly said from what was actually visible. Automated player ratings are statistical summaries, not an independent eye test. Avoid reviewing only disappointing owned players while accepting a target's numbers uncritically.
2. **Extract specific claims with AI.** Record who observed what, the fixture, publication time, supporting passage or clip timestamp, direction of change, uncertainty and contradictions. Examples: repeatedly dropping outside the box; failing to attack crosses; being bypassed by teammates; slower recovery runs; a competitor taking the central role; explicitly losing penalty duties. An insult alone creates a review flag, not a numerical adjustment. Reports of apparent low confidence remain subjective observations, not psychological diagnoses.
3. **Check independence and relevance.** Syndicated articles, podcast quotes and reposts count as the same underlying observation. Several accounts repeating a missed chance do not create independent evidence of declining ability. Distinguish one matchup from a repeated pattern and separate specific player observations from general team criticism. Missing coverage means unknown, not reassuring.
4. **Translate observations into football mechanisms.** A deeper role changes expected box involvement and non-penalty xG; loss of penalties changes taker probability; credible competition changes start probability and minutes. Technique concerns can inform a separately shrunk finishing estimate, while chances spoiled before a shot may indicate chance-production problems absent from shot-based xG. A missed chance already visible in the quantitative inputs should not earn an additional generic negative sentiment penalty.
5. **Make disagreement visible and actionable.** Show the statistical baseline, scouting evidence, a revised forecast or plausible scenarios, and the transfer decision under each. If persuasive scouting contradicts a small hold advantage, the answer should be “hold depends on this assumption,” with a concrete replacement comparison. A human review can change a decision immediately when the evidence warrants it; the model is an aid, not a veto.
6. **Measure incremental value.** Freeze claims before deadlines and compare statistics-only versus statistics-plus-scouting predictions. Grade starts/minutes and future chance creation separately from realised points, then evaluate the resulting transfer policy. Learn credibility by source and claim type, with shrinkage for small samples. A language model's confidence is not a calibrated forecast probability. Do not invent a universal 70/30 stats-versus-eye-test weighting.

Initial claim record:

```
player / fixture / observed_at / published_at
observation / evidence_url / passage_or_clip_timestamp
observer / source_type / independence_group
affected_mechanism / counterevidence / confidence_basis
already_in_numeric_inputs / review_or_expiry_event
baseline_forecast / scenario_or_adjustment / decision_effect
```

## Demonstration: could this actually change the decision?

I ran an explicitly hypothetical stress test on the frozen September 6 recency forecasts. It reduces only the owned player's expected chance-creation rates, leaving minutes and all alternatives unchanged. It is a sensitivity test, not an estimate learned from the reports above. The baseline and adjusted comparisons use the same legal squad evaluator, no hits and GW4–9.

| Swap | No additional downgrade | 10% lower chance creation for six GWs | 20% lower for six GWs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Thiago → João Pedro | +0.11 | +1.61 | +3.10 |
| Kluivert → Barnes | +0.66 | +1.89 | +3.13 |
| Kluivert → Tavernier | −0.08 | +1.16 | +2.39 |

These are static six-week squad-point differences, not advantages over a future plan that can transfer next week. The scenarios are asymmetric tests of the owned players; replacement candidates need the same scouting scrutiny. They show that the hold conclusion is sensitive enough for credible new football evidence to matter. They do not justify selecting a convenient downgrade to force a desired sale.

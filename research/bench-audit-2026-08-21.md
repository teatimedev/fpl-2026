# FPL GW1 minutes and bench audit — 21 August 2026

## Bottom line

- The defender is **Malick Thiaw**, not Tyrell Malacia. His 1.7% ownership is not a warning that he will rarely play: he started 33 league matches last season, played 2,963 minutes, and is predicted to start against Liverpool by both current line-up sources. He is a sound minutes pick, although Newcastle's difficult first fixture reduces his immediate clean-sheet appeal.
- **Phil Foden** is a calculated differential rather than a mistake. His 2025/26 output was only 131 FPL points from 23 starts, but the new-manager evidence is encouraging: he captained Enzo Maresca's first pre-season XI and started both the K-League friendly and the Community Shield. His upside at £7.0m is substantial; the risk is substitution and rotation rather than a likely GW1 benching.
- **Justin Kluivert** is the least secure of the three. Both current predicted-line-up sources start him, and his three-goal/one-assist pre-season plus likely set-piece role explain the punt. But the primary evidence is weaker: he managed only 10 league starts and 945 minutes last season and ended it as a substitute. Treat him as high-variance first-sub/rotation cover, not a nailed player.
- The bench concern is valid. Leno and O'Shea are credible starters; Reed is not, Destan is definitely unusable, and no £4.5m forward has convincing starter-level minutes. The present bench is too fragile if it is left unchanged.

## Malick Thiaw

The minutes case is strong. A 33-start, 2,963-minute league season is close to first-choice-centre-back usage. Newcastle also made him captain for the Valencia friendly; he played 72 minutes. Newcastle's official profile describes a solid first season at the club, and current predicted-line-up aggregators put him in both the FFScout and Rotowire XIs.

The 1.7% ownership is more plausibly explained by context than by minutes:

- Newcastle open at home to Liverpool, followed by Spurs away. Those are unattractive clean-sheet fixtures.
- At £5.0m he competes with cheaper £4.0m–£4.5m defenders and more fashionable attacking defenders.
- He still has upside: four league goals and 12 bonus points last season, and one pre-season goal this summer.

Verdict: **keep and start if accepting the difficult fixture**. The local model estimates an 86.6% start probability and 78 minutes, with 4.36 points in GW1 and 29.32 over six weeks. The exact numbers are uncertain, but the role conclusion is well supported.

## Phil Foden

The concern about last season is fair, but it needs calibrating. Foden scored 131 FPL points from 23 starts and 2,078 minutes. City's own season reporting credits him with seven league goals during the campaign and 16 league goal involvements by May. That was below his 2023/24 peak, but it was not a complete collapse.

The selection thesis is based on role and price:

- He costs only £7.0m because the previous season and rotation risk are already priced in.
- Maresca made him captain in his first pre-season match and used him among the three attacking midfielders behind the striker.
- Foden started the K-League friendly, played 60 minutes, and started the Community Shield, again playing 60.
- Both current predicted-line-up sources put him in the GW1 XI at home to Bournemouth.

The downside remains genuine: City's squad is crowded, Foden was routinely withdrawn around 60 minutes in the final two warm-ups, and a new coach means the exact hierarchy is not fully proven. The model's 77.1% start probability and 68 projected minutes reflect that.

Verdict: **a defensible high-upside differential at £7.0m, but not a safe 90-minute player**. Low ownership is part of the upside, not the reason to buy him; the reason is the likely central attacking role plus price. If maximum minutes certainty is the priority, he is one of the first starters to reconsider.

## Justin Kluivert

This is a real gamble. The positive case is:

- Both FFScout and Rotowire currently predict him to start at Manchester City.
- He produced three goals and one assist across pre-season.
- The most recent published set-piece hierarchy has him first for Bournemouth penalties and involved on direct free-kicks/corners.
- At £6.0m, those routes to points make him a potentially useful first substitute once the fixtures improve.

But the negative evidence matters more for reliability: only 10 starts, 945 minutes and 41 FPL points last season; he came on in the 73rd minute on the final day; and Bournemouth now have a new coach, Marco Rose. There is no current official club statement guaranteeing Kluivert's role. Bournemouth also start away to Manchester City and then face Everton, Newcastle, Brentford, Liverpool and Chelsea in their first six.

Verdict: **bench him in GW1 and use him as first substitute if retaining him**. He is a sensible upside punt, but low ownership is justified and he should not be counted as completely nailed cover. The local model estimates an 83.2% start probability and 68 minutes, but that estimate is more confident than the primary evidence alone.

## Bench audit

Assuming O'Shea starts over Kluivert, the saved bench becomes **Leno; Kluivert, Reed, Destan**.

- **Leno:** genuinely nailed — 38 starts and 3,420 minutes last season. He is excellent insurance but, because Raya is also secure, the £0.5m above a minimum goalkeeper is unlikely to be used often.
- **Kluivert:** current consensus starter but role-risky; credible first substitute.
- **O'Shea:** if left on the bench, he is actually credible cover. Both current sources predict him at right-back and he recorded two goals and one assist in pre-season. The local model has him at 88.5% to start and 79 minutes.
- **Reed:** essentially dead as FPL cover. One league start and 89 minutes last season; current predicted XIs omit him. The model gives him only 37.9% to start and 32 minutes.
- **Destan:** definitively dead. He has left Hull and is marked unavailable by FPL.

There is no fully nailed £4.5m forward in the current pool. Obi is the model's best £4.5m forward, but at only a 25.2% estimated start probability and 21 projected minutes he is merely a budget-valid replacement for Destan, not dependable cover.

## Safer, budget-legal structure preserving the Haaland core

The smallest functional improvement is **Leno (£4.5m) to Steele (£4.0m), Reed (£4.5m) to Sadiki (£5.0m), and Destan to Obi at the same £4.5m price**. Sadiki started 33 times and played 2,891 league minutes last season; both current line-up sources predict him to start for Sunderland. This deliberately gives up reserve-goalkeeper cover to obtain an outfield substitute who can actually auto-sub. With Raya projected for 86 minutes and a 95.7% start rate, that is a reasonable FPL trade-off, although it does mean there is no goalkeeper safety net. A 50,000-run local simulation improved the squad by about 1.36 points over six weeks, which is useful but not decisive.

That minimum-change bench would be **Steele; Kluivert, Sadiki, Obi**. Kluivert and Sadiki are credible cover; Steele and Obi are still enablers.

If the objective is to keep a playing second goalkeeper as well, use the fuller reshuffle below.

The £15.0m outlay is unchanged. With O'Shea in the starting XI, the bench becomes **Leno; Kluivert, Ampadu, Hirst**. That gives two credible outfield substitutes and a third with at least a plausible route to minutes. It also retains Raya, Calafiori, Thiaw, Tarkowski, Saka, Mbeumo, Foden, Haaland and Thiago.

This is a **minutes-security** recommendation, not a claim that every replacement has higher attacking upside. Mitchell/Ampadu/Hirst project to about 59 combined points across six weeks in the local model versus roughly 38 for Guéhi/Reed/Destan, but bench points only matter when an automatic substitute is required. The most conservative smaller move is simply Destan to Obi; it fixes an invalid player but does not solve the fragility.
